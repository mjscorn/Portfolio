Old Man

Where have you been? I never knew a razor blade could cut like that, was meant to drag across my tan faced youth and turn me into a man like leaves turning from green to gold, from brown to dust, into petrol to get home.

What death have you died? Did bullets scuttle through your hull and riddle your skin in muddy fields of Saigon? Was it the sun setting on your lingering days behind cornfields so lush and so green or over husks dried out by the heat from your porch at night slowly rocking to sleep?

For now I’ll pretend you’re something you’re not —a painter or war hero, a violist for the Met. Or even a Bog Willow whose roots stretch beneath, slowly inching you closer. Closer and closer to me.

In a World Only Humming

There is a floating dock in the middle of a lake. College girls lie there. They seem listless and unaware of the world that’s happening all around them—boys wrestle off, boats drift by, wakes slowly pass. Tomorrow they will be lying there. Yesterday they were lying there. The dock was made with them lying there, shipped to the Sierras with them lying there —all pale skin and sunglasses and trees silhouetted by a burning drop of light.

When they make it back to shore their skin will be wrinkled, their loved ones long gone, and the dock will have rotted. The world will be steel and cogs entire with blinking things a thousand stories high. They will shiver, forgetting where on shore they left their clothes. They will huddle close for warmth, thighs against thighs, arms around chests, and cry electric tears.

College girls. Lying on a floating dock in the middle of a lake.

QuickBooks Mobile

Where we started

When this project began, the team had a fairly large charter: find opportunities to increase the payments processing volume on QuickBooks mobile.

So we launched into research mode and started talking to customers. From what we heard, we started iterating, prototyping concepts, and refining ideas with their input. These early “follow me homes,” comic strip readings, feature purchasing exercises, and low fidelity prototypes were what ultimately shaped our future roadmaps and launches.

What we learned

What testing surfaced about how merchants actually take payments on mobile:

Payments is a primary mobile use case, but wasn't reflected in the IA (which was more accounting-first focused)
Users couldn't take a quick payment and expected they could, so were supplementing our app with other tools (which was causing double the work and time)
When customers went to pay an open invoice, the workflow buried critical features and asked questions in an order counter to most payment mental-models
How?Who?What?Method?

What we did about it

So the team restructured the QuickBooks Mobile app (which in a large company where you are one vertical team contributing to an ownership team with different incentives, isn't always easy). What we did was:

Made payments a primary navigation item to reflect customers' mental models
Created a new, simplified workflow to let customers take a quick payment without needing to create an invoice
When paying an invoice, customers' mental models were now reflected in the IA where all the once-buried payments features were now exposed in one consolidated, scalable screen

Impact

We shipped these new experiences on iOS & Android and overall saw:

$3.7M in incremental annualized revenue
The newly added Sales tab become the most visited screen in QuickBooks mobile app today
Our newer customers have higher engagement as measured by total payments charge volume (or TPV)
4 new components contributed to design systems
